Text hook:
“God, I’m walking into a shift 
Where one mistake could cost my license.

I don’t know what to do next”

Caption:
Sometimes at the start of my shift—when I see how much care my patients need— the pre-shift anxiety hits hard.
Sometimes I fear I won’t be able to handle it.
Sometimes I fear I’ll make a mistake so big it could cost my license.
Sometimes I fear I won’t be able to keep my cool and I’ll snap at my coworkers.
Sometimes I fear I’ll disappoint my patients.
And sometimes… I fear I might harm someone through my actions.
“God, I’m walking into a shift where one mistake could cost someone’s life”
But this is what I’ve learned:
I don’t walk into my shift alone.
I surrender my fears.
And I trust that the Lord will make a way—even when I can’t see it yet.
📖 “When I am afraid, I put my trust in You.” — Psalm 56:3
Today, I just finished six shifts straight.
I was anxious on day one… but here I am—standing strong.
My patients are safe. And I’m still here. Praise God. 🙏
Trust. In. The. Lord.
⏩ Send this to a nurse who might need to hear this. 
And if you’re a nurse who has pre-shift anxiety, how do you usually deal with it? 👇

Notes:
Execution
1. Text Hook
	•	Write a short and honest prayer or inner monologue that expresses a fear or uncertainty you carry into your work
	•	Make sure the emotion and wording naturally lead into the word "Trust" as the resolution after the beat drop
	•	Keep it raw and unpolished, the vulnerability is what makes this format work
2. Caption
	•	Open by describing the anxiety or fear you feel before a shift in honest and specific terms
	•	Build toward a moment of surrender and faith, ending with a clear and powerful closing statement
	•	Add CTA to encourage viewers to share it with someone who needs to hear it, and close with an engagement question
3. B-roll
	•	Film b-roll that directly related to your point and the worry that you have
	•	If you work 9-5, film at your desk while looking stressed out
